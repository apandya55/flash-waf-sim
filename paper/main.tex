\begin{abstract}
We measured page-program amplification in a simplified page-mapped flash translation layer simulator at 40\%, 70\%, and 85\% mapped-page occupancy. Each run prepopulated all active logical addresses, applied 5,000 warm-up updates, and measured the next 5,000 updates. Thirty paired pseudorandom seed blocks covered six occupancy-by-workload conditions, for 180 condition runs. The paired hot/cold-minus-uniform differences were +0.32987 at 40\% occupancy (95\% descriptive paired Student-$t$ interval [0.32665, 0.33310]), +0.60656 at 70\% ([0.60054, 0.61258]), and +0.80465 at 85\% ([0.78676, 0.82253]); all intervals use $df=29$. The sign reversal reported in the separate start-empty baseline did not appear under this protocol. These results characterize one simulator and synthetic workload design, not physical NAND devices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Flash translation layers map host logical addresses to physical NAND pages. Garbage collection moves valid pages out of blocks that contain invalid pages, then erases those blocks. The copying work contributes to write amplification, and its amount depends on the workload and collection policy. Prior studies analyze write amplification, greedy collection, valid-page distributions, and hot/cold or locality-aware FTL policies \cite{hu2009waf,agrawal2008design,bux2010greedy,peleato2012valid,agarwal2010closed,vanhoudt2013hotcold,chen2019hcftl,wang2015datacentric,kang2015hotpages,park2019model}. We use a compact software model to characterize program amplification under two synthetic full-page overwrite distributions at equal mapped-page occupancy.

\section{Related work}
Hu et al. analyze write amplification in flash-based SSDs, while Agrawal et al. describe mapping and garbage-collection trade-offs \cite{hu2009waf,agrawal2008design}. Bux and Iliadis analyze greedy collection. Peleato et al. characterize valid-page distributions under greedy GC, and Agarwal and Marrow derive a closed-form NAND amplification expression \cite{bux2010greedy,peleato2012valid,agarwal2010closed}. Other studies examine hot/cold data, locality-aware page-level FTLs, data-centric collection, and policies based on hot-page ratios \cite{vanhoudt2013hotcold,chen2019hcftl,wang2015datacentric,kang2015hotpages}. Park et al. present another write-amplification model \cite{park2019model}. This study contributes a reproducible observation about this implementation and workload construction. It does not establish a general utilization-locality law.

\section{Simulation model}
The simulator represents each physical page as free, valid, or invalid. An in-memory dictionary stores logical-to-physical mappings. A host write programs one free physical page and invalidates the previous page for that logical address. When free pages reach the configured threshold, greedy garbage collection selects a block with the most invalid pages, copies its remaining valid pages, and erases it. Ties favor the block with the smaller erase count. Page allocation prefers the current block and otherwise selects a block with the most free pages. The model excludes persistent mapping metadata, NAND timing, parallel channels, bad blocks, ECC, static wear leveling, failures, and controller behavior.

\section{Experimental method}
We modeled 16 blocks of 32 pages each, for 512 physical pages, with 4,096-byte pages and a GC threshold of 32 free pages. Active logical address counts were 205, 358, and 435 pages, representing 40\%, 70\%, and 85\% of physical capacity after rounding. Each condition began by writing every active LBA once in ascending order. This set the mapped and valid page counts to the active-page count before traffic.

Each of 30 seed blocks ran all six occupancy-by-workload conditions, for 180 runs total. We used NumPy's \texttt{default\_rng} with seeds 202601 through 202630. For a given seed, all conditions used the same three latent uniform random arrays. Uniform traffic sampled the active range uniformly. Hot/cold traffic directed 80\% of updates to a hot set containing 10\% of active addresses and 20\% to the disjoint cold remainder. The address sequences differ between conditions because the latent draws are transformed according to each workload distribution. Seed blocks are the independent replication units for paired contrasts.

Each run applied 5,000 warm-up updates and then 5,000 measured updates. The simulator checked its mapping and page-state invariants after warm-up and measurement. Mapped and valid page counts remained equal to the configured active-page count in all runs. Measurement counters were computed as differences from the counters at the warm-up boundary. Thus the reported outcome excludes warm-up host programs, GC copies, and erasures. The fixed warm-up defines the experiment; we did not test whether the simulator had converged to steady state.

Let $H$ be the measured host page programs and $G$ the measured valid-page GC copy programs. Program amplification is $(H+G)/H$, in modeled physical page programs per host page program. It excludes metadata and other device work. We computed hot/cold-minus-uniform differences within each seed block and differences-in-differences between occupancy levels. Two-sided descriptive 95\% paired Student-$t$ intervals use $df=29$. They describe variation among the 30 pseudorandom seed blocks under the fixed model and workload generator. They do not support inference about real devices or a population of workloads.

\section{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:results} reports cell means and sample standard deviations. Hot/cold measured amplification exceeded uniform amplification at each occupancy. Paired hot/cold-minus-uniform differences were +0.32987 at 40\% (95\% interval [0.32665, 0.33310]), +0.60656 at 70\% ([0.60054, 0.61258]), and +0.80465 at 85\% ([0.78676, 0.82253]). The differences-in-differences relative to 40\% were +0.27669 at 70\% ([0.27049, 0.28289]) and +0.47477 at 85\% ([0.45711, 0.49244]). All intervals have $df=29$. The paired contrast increased across the three tested occupancy conditions in this simulator.

The earlier start-empty experiment is archived separately in the directory \url{research/results/baseline_start_empty_5seed/}. It reported positive hot/cold-minus-uniform amplification at 40\% and negative contrasts at 70\% and 85\%. Under the revised prepopulation, warm-up, and measured-phase protocol, that sign reversal did not recur. The studies differ in initial state, phase definition, and number of seed blocks, so their numerical estimates are not pooled.

\subsection{Sensitivity to workload, geometry, and warm-up}
At 70\% occupancy, we ran a separate one-factor-at-a-time sensitivity grid with ten paired seed blocks per condition and 5,000 measured updates. The hot/cold 80/10-minus-uniform contrast was +0.60068 (95\% paired Student-$t$ interval [0.58968, 0.61168], $df=9$) in the base 16-by-32 geometry after 5,000 warm-up updates. Across warm-up lengths of 0, 2,500, 5,000, and 10,000 updates, the mean contrast ranged from +0.50408 to +0.60486. In the 32-by-32 geometry it was +0.57378. Changing the workload produced contrasts of +1.00414 for hot/cold 90/5 and +0.77954 for a Zipf distribution with exponent 1.2. All tested contrasts remained positive, while their magnitude varied with workload and design. These ten-seed checks are descriptive and are separate from the 30-seed primary experiment.

\section{Discussion}
The revised experiment compares workload distributions after mapping the same number of active logical pages and applying an explicit warm-up. Hot/cold traffic had higher measured amplification at all three occupancies, and the paired contrast increased with occupancy. This differs from the start-empty baseline, where the contrast changed sign. The baseline did not hold realized occupancy constant during traffic and mixed initialization with measurement. The reversal was not robust to the revised protocol. The experiment does not identify which design choice accounts for the change, because prepopulation, warm-up, measurement separation, and seed count changed together.

The sensitivity grid shows that the positive hot/cold effect persists for the tested workload, geometry, and warm-up variants, although its size changes. The primary experiment's increasing contrast across occupancy therefore describes only its chosen workload and simulator configuration. The tested variations do not establish a general utilization-locality relationship.

Greedy GC and workload locality have extensive prior study. This experiment does not claim novelty for a general interaction between locality and occupancy. It documents how a bounded implementation behaved under a specified synthetic workload after controlling the mapped-page population. The fixed warm-up and one measured interval do not establish steady-state performance. Further work would need varied workload constructions, geometries, warm-up lengths, and independently validated models before making broader claims.

\section{Limitations}
The simulator is a software abstraction of a page-mapped FTL. It lacks physical NAND behavior, metadata programs, controller caches, timing, channel parallelism, wear-leveling migration, endurance failures, and device validation. The primary experiment tests three occupancies, two synthetic distributions, one geometry, one warm-up length, and one measurement length. The separate sensitivity grid varies workload family, geometry, and warm-up at 70\% occupancy, but tests only ten seed blocks per cell and one measurement length. Neither experiment establishes warm-up convergence or covers real workload populations. The intervals summarize pseudorandom seed variation under fixed designs and do not represent uncertainty across hardware or workload populations. Erase counts are simulated operations and do not estimate device lifetime.

\section{Conclusion}
After prepopulating active logical addresses and separating warm-up from measurement, the original sign reversal was absent. Hot/cold-minus-uniform measured program-amplification contrasts were +0.32987, +0.60656, and +0.80465 at 40\%, 70\%, and 85\% mapped occupancy. The contrast increased across these tested conditions. Sensitivity checks retained a positive contrast in the tested variants, with different magnitudes. These results document a bounded behavior of this simulator and workload generator. They do not establish a general research contribution or predict behavior on commercial NAND devices.
