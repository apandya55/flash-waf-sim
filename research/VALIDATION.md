# Validation record

## Protocol and generated results

The revised raw data contain 180 condition runs: six workload-by-occupancy cells across 30 seed blocks. Each seed runs every condition, and all conditions with the same seed use the same three latent random arrays before treatment-specific mapping. Each run prepopulates the full active LBA set, checks that mapped and valid page counts equal the configured occupancy, performs 5,000 warm-up updates, and measures the next 5,000 updates. Raw output records phase lengths, occupancy counts, measured host programs, GC copies, erasures, and final state metrics. All trials reported exactly 5,000 measured host page programs and maintained their configured mapped and valid page counts.

The outcome is recomputed from raw counters as `(host_programs + gc_copy_programs) / host_programs`. The summary script reconstructs paired contrasts by seed and uses two-sided 95% Student-t intervals with df=29. Results are +0.32987 [0.32665, 0.33310] at 40%, +0.60656 [0.60054, 0.61258] at 70%, and +0.80465 [0.78676, 0.82253] at 85% occupancy. Difference-in-differences relative to 40% are +0.27669 [0.27049, 0.28289] at 70%, and +0.47477 [0.45711, 0.49244] at 85%. Intervals describe seed-stream variation for this fixed simulator and generated workload only.

The original start-empty five-seed results have been retained in `research/results/baseline_start_empty_5seed/`; they are not pooled with the revised results. The baseline's apparent sign reversal is absent under the revised protocol. Since the revision changed prepopulation, warm-up separation, measurement phase, and seed count together, this experiment cannot attribute the difference to one individual change.

## Software checks

The unit suite validates page-state conservation, mapping consistency through garbage collection, erase accounting, buffer packing mechanics, and research-trial occupancy/counter boundaries. These checks assess software bookkeeping. They do not validate electrical NAND behavior or the scientific adequacy of the model. The previous test comparing buffer and unbuffered WAF on separately generated random traces was replaced with a narrow packing test because those traces did not represent equivalent logical workloads.

The project declares package discovery explicitly in `pyproject.toml` so a fresh editable install does not fail on the repository's flat layout. Reproduction is verified by installing `.[dev]` in a clean virtual environment, running pytest, regenerating the 180 trials and summary, and comparing generated files for deterministic reproducibility.

## Bounded sensitivity checks and analytical reference

The follow-up sensitivity grid contains 120 runs: ten paired seed blocks for each of 12 cells at 70% mapped occupancy. The base design uses 16 blocks of 32 pages, 5,000 warm-up updates, and 5,000 measured updates. The grid varies workload family, geometry, or warm-up length one factor at a time. At the base design, the mean paired hot/cold 80/10-minus-uniform contrast is +0.60068 [0.58968, 0.61168]. Across the tested warm-up lengths (0, 2,500, 5,000, and 10,000), the mean contrast ranges from +0.50408 to +0.60486. At 32-by-32 geometry it is +0.57378. Alternative workload contrasts are +1.00414 [0.98828, 1.02000] for hot/cold 90/5 and +0.77954 [0.76186, 0.79722] for Zipf exponent 1.2. The listed intervals are paired Student-t intervals with df=9 and describe these fixed seed blocks only. Every paired contrast remains positive in these ten-seed checks. Values depend on workload family and design choices; these checks do not establish a population effect. The robustness figure reports cell means and standard deviations, and the raw runs are in `research/results/robustness_trials.json`.

The uniform-workload means rise with mapped occupancy (1.15252, 2.16984, and 5.06177 at 40%, 70%, and 85%). This direction is consistent with the over-provisioning trend analyzed by Agarwal and Marrow (2010) for uniform random writes and greedy garbage collection. It is a qualitative reference check, not a numerical validation: the simulator does not reproduce the analytical model's steady-state assumptions. DOI metadata and abstract scope were checked against Crossref and OpenAlex. This review did not establish novelty. Prior work already covers write amplification, greedy collection, hot/cold workloads, and locality-aware FTL and GC policies. The current evidence supports an implementation-specific software characterization, with no demonstrated general research contribution.

The original sign reversal remains specific to the archived start-empty five-seed baseline. It is absent in the prepopulated protocol and in the bounded sensitivity grid. The protocol changes were made together in the revised primary experiment, so the cause of the difference from baseline is unresolved. The ten-seed sensitivity cells are exploratory robustness checks and are not pooled with the 30-seed primary experiment.

## Scope and remaining scientific limits

The simulator omits persistent metadata, NAND timing, parallel channels, bad blocks, ECC, static wear leveling, endurance failures, and hardware validation. The primary study uses one geometry, one warm-up length, one measurement length, and two synthetic workloads. The sensitivity sprint adds selected workload, geometry, and warm-up variants at one occupancy, but uses only ten paired seed blocks per cell. Neither protocol demonstrates steady-state convergence or represents real workload populations. The observed contrasts are not evidence for physical SSD behavior.
